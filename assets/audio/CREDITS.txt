Audio provenance for 三更：殭屍 / The Third Watch: The Jiangshi
===============================================================

Twelve files, all CC0, listed in full below. Every cue NOT listed here is still
synthesised in js/audio.js from oscillators and a noise buffer — see the cue
table at the bottom for which is which.

If recorded cues are added, each one MUST be listed below before it ships, and
this list must stay complete — including for public-domain and CC0 files, where
no attribution is legally required. The point is provenance: a future
contributor has to be able to verify where every file came from without
trusting anyone's memory.

Licence rules for this repo (it is public):
  - CC0 / public domain preferred.
  - CC-BY is acceptable ONLY if credits.html also names author, licence and any
    changes made.
  - "Free for personal use" and similar non-commercial terms are NOT acceptable.

Format, one block per file:

  file:     <name in this directory>
  cue:      <the manifest key it answers to>
  source:   <URL the file came from>
  author:   <name as the source gives it>
  licence:  <CC0 / CC-BY-4.0 / Pixabay / ...>
  changes:  <trimmed, normalised, converted, or "none">
  added:    <YYYY-MM-DD>

The held sounds — wind bed, pack murmur, scare sting
----------------------------------------------------
Sourced from OpenGameArt, CC0, and re-encoded rather than used as-is: the
originals are 0.8-1.6 MB each, well over the 300 KB an ambient loop is allowed
here. Each was decoded, cut, and re-encoded to mono Opus at 24 kHz in a
browser, which is the only encoder available in this toolchain.

The two loops are seamless by construction: the tail of the clip is crossfaded
back over its own head, so the join has no edge to hear. A plain trim would
click once a bar.

How the manifest works
----------------------
js/audio.js fetches assets/audio/manifest.json and, for each "cue": "file"
entry, decodes the file and plays it in place of the synthesised version. No
manifest, a missing file, or a decode failure all fall back to synthesis, so
the game never waits on audio and never goes silent.

Cue keys the code understands
-----------------------------
A key here is a name js/audio.js will look for in the manifest. A key with no
manifest entry is not a gap — it means that cue is synthesised, which is the
default and never a failure state.

  door        a hinge giving way, on entering a room
  sting       the pack arriving
  hop         殭屍 landing, both feet together
  hit         a blow landing in combat
  bell        the hour striking eleven
  drum        更鼓, the watch drum
  paper       符咒 — a talisman found, or spent
  king        殭屍王 arriving at 三更
  break       them coming through a wall
  cower       a held breath let go
  relic       the 神主牌 found
  seam        crossing the 月門 between the house and the village
  win         the burial done
  death       the run lost
  item-metal  picking up 戒刀 / 銅錢劍 / 七星劍
  item-wood   picking up 桃木劍
  item-liquid picking up 黑狗血
  item-small  picking up 硃砂 / 糯米 / 金丹
  step-wood   walking indoors, on boards  (a list of takes, chosen between)
  step-grass  walking outdoors, on earth  (a list of takes, chosen between)
  card        the event window arriving
  tick        landing on a doorway with the keyboard
  swing-metal a blade landing    (layered over `hit`)
  swing-wood  a wooden weapon landing (layered over `hit`)

The talismans and the 攝魂幡 have no item cue of their own: they route to
`paper`, because picking up a talisman and throwing one are the same material
doing the same thing, and giving them two sounds would be inventing a
distinction the fiction does not have.

`item-engine` and `swing-saw` are gone with the petrol saw that needed them.

The two held sounds — the wind bed and the pack murmur — take no cue key and
cannot be replaced by a file. They are generated, they loop by construction,
and the bed follows the clock: it thickens as the night draws on, which a
recording of wind cannot do.

Which cues are recorded, and which are synthesised
--------------------------------------------------
recorded:     door, break, hit, item-metal, item-wood, item-small,
              step-wood, step-grass, sting, bed-wind, murmur
synthesised:  drum, hop, paper, king, bell, cower, relic, seam, win, death,
              item-liquid, card, tick, wick, the two swing layers, the murmur's
              growl voices (which sit under the recorded bed), and the whole
              break-in cue set — the accelerating pounding, the splinter cracks
              and the debris tail under the collapse.

The four jiangshi cues are synthesised on purpose, not for want of a pack, and
three of them could not honestly be bought:

  drum   一更/二更/三更 is a specific instrument struck a specific NUMBER of
         times, and the count is the information. A single recording would fix
         the rhythm between strikes forever; the code strikes it two at ten,
         three at eleven, and once more at 三更 — the last one anybody out
         there will strike, which is what makes the silence after it mean
         something.
  hop    a hop is a rhythm, not an impact. What identifies a 殭屍 is that the
         legs have set and both feet land together, and then the grave clothes
         arrive a beat behind. That is three envelopes with a relationship
         between them, and a library file is one envelope and the room it was
         recorded in.
  paper  a talisman is a piece of paper moving fast. Three short bursts, because
         a flutter is several edges close together and one burst is a hiss.
  king   deliberately the inverse of the pack's sting. That one RISES: a pack is
         coming and the question is how fast. This one FALLS. He is not hurrying
         and there is nothing to decide about whether he arrives.

Each of the four still checks the manifest first, exactly as every other cue
does, so any of them can be replaced by a recording later without a caller
changing. None of them appears in manifest.json today — a key naming a file
that does not exist would 404 on every load and buy nothing.

Calm mode
---------
Calm does not silence these. It takes away the assault — the faces, the blood,
the buzzing — and keeps the information, which is the same policy the scare
sting has always followed: a player who cannot see the pack still has to hear
that there is one, and how many, and which wall.

What it does do is take the bite out. `hop` and `king` scale to 60%, the bed
sits lower, the score halves, and the duck after a scare is held longer. The
drum and the paper are not assault and are not touched.

The rest of the synthesised set is not an oversight. They are sounds these packs
cannot serve honestly: a held breath, a tablet found, a world changing
temperature. Something close-ish would be worse than a synth, which at least was
written for the moment it plays in.
