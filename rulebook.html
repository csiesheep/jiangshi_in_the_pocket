The night

You are the caretaker. The village has locked its doors, and at the far end of it is the corpse hostel, where coffins wait for the road home. One of them has waited too long.

It is 9:00 PM. At midnight the third watch sounds and the thing in that coffin comes for you. You have until then to do one of two things:

  1. Lay him to rest. Find his ancestral tablet in the Sealed Crypt, carry it out of the village, and bury it in the Mass Grave.
  2. Meet him. Be standing there properly armed when he arrives — and it is only what you are holding that decides it.

Either ends the night. So does dying.

What you track

Twenty tiles, ten indoor and ten outdoor. An event pool and an item pool, kept separate. Thirteen items, in five kinds.

Three numbers: Health, Attack, and the turn you are on.

Setup

  1. The Gatehouse goes down first. The Back Steps is set aside — it goes down later, the moment you first step outside.
  2. The rest are shuffled into two face-down stacks, indoor and outdoor.
  3. You start with Attack 0, Health 10, and three sticky rice. Bare-handed really is nothing: the sword you find IS your attack, never a bonus on top of one.
  4. The clock reads 9:00 PM. It is turn 1 of 30.

You begin in the Gatehouse and draw no event for it. The night starts when you move.

The clock

The turn is the clock. There is no deck to run down.

The game is exactly 30 turns, each of them six minutes. Turn 1 begins at nine; turn 30 ends at midnight. The three hour bands are ten turns each.

BandTurnsClock
9 PM1–1021:00 → 22:00
10 PM11–2022:00 → 23:00
11 PM21–3023:00 → 24:00

Events get worse as the bands advance. There is no losing to the clock — midnight is not a deadline you fail, it is an appointment you keep.

Taking a turn

Every turn is one action, then an event, then an optional search.

1 — Choose one action

MOVE — step into an adjacent room, explored or not. If it is unexplored, a tile goes down.

STAY — remain where you are. Legal, and often correct: staying is how you search a room a second time.

Movement is optional — but standing still costs a turn and draws an event exactly like walking does. There is no free turn.

2 — Place the tile, if the room is new

The top tile of the matching stack goes down. Rotated however it likes, with one constraint: one of its ways out must line up with the way you just came through. Its other edges match nothing in particular — a door may open onto a blank wall, and a wall may seal off a neighbour's door.

There is no fixed footprint. The village sprawls as far as the tiles take it.

3 — Draw an event

One event, read for the current hour band. Always — including when you re-enter a room you know, and including when you stayed put.

4 — Search, if the room offers it

Ten of the twenty places can be searched, each for one category of thing.

CategoryWhere
WeaponsBlacksmith · Woodshed · Memorial Arch
TalismansSutra Hall · Mourning Hall · Bamboo Grove
MedicineApothecary · Counting Room · Pagoda Tree
The implementEarth God Shrine — and nowhere else

Searching is free — it costs no turn. It happens after the event, and it may find nothing. If it does, you can search the same room again by spending next turn on STAY — which means another event. That is the price of rummaging: not the search, the lingering.

You may carry 4 items. Neither the ancestral tablet nor what is in your hands counts against that.

5 — End of turn

Incense Hall and Pagoda Tree give +1 Health if you finish your turn standing in them. Then the clock advances six minutes.

Poisoned

corpse-poison gets into you, and it does not wear off.

−1 Health at the start of every turn, until a sticky rice draws it out.

Nothing else cures it, and it does not stack — poisoned is poisoned, however often it happens. The Protective Charm does not help: poison is not a wound, and the charm only softens wounds.

Left alone it will kill you. The rice in your pack is the only answer — which is exactly why you can never comfortably spend it on anything else.

A villager is hurt

Someone from the village is bitten and bleeding. Spend a sticky rice to draw the poison out of them, and they give you what they have. Refuse — or have no rice — and you watch them turn, and then you fight them.

The rice you keep for yourself is rice you don't have when someone needs it — and late in the night the villagers are carrying exactly what you were searching for. Rice is also the only thing that cures poison. Poisoned, with one sticky rice left and a bleeding villager in front of you, is the hardest choice the game asks.

Fighting, and not fighting

No dice. The arithmetic is the whole of it:

Health lost = (number of jiangshi) − (your Attack)

Never more than 4 from one fight, and never a gain. That ceiling is why a very high Attack stops helping against an ordinary pack — you can only get the damage down to zero.

Running. On any jiangshi you may run instead of fighting: −1 Health, and you step into an adjacent room you have already been in. You draw no event there and you do not search — the turn is over. Because you are no longer standing in the room you fled, a dead end there will not break its wall on you. Spending Black Dog Blood does the same thing for free, which is what the blood is for.

Protective Charm softens jiangshi wounds by 1 and nothing else — not the Lose Health events, not poison, not the Health that running costs.

The breach — when the wall gives

If a room leaves you with no unexplored way out, they come through the wall once the room's own event has been dealt with — three at nine o'clock, four at ten, five at eleven. You choose which wall.

So a dead end can cost you two fights in one turn, back to back. Late at night that is the worst thing that can happen to you in a single turn. The hole stays open afterwards, and you can use it.

The warded stone

The stone at the junction is cut with its own name, and it means it. No event is drawn on the Stone Ward — not when you walk onto it, and not when you stay.

It is the only ground in the village that is quiet, and it is the whole of the game's safety. There is nothing to find there and nothing to heal you: standing on it costs a turn and buys you the draw you did not make. Late in the night, when most draws are a fight, that is the best trade available — and the reason it is not simply free is that the stone is somewhere you have to get to, and it is outdoors.

Safety in this game is a place, not a thing you carry. You cannot take the stone with you, you cannot save it for later, and while you are standing on it you are not walking towards the grave.

He still comes there at midnight. Nowhere refuses him — the stone turns the night away, and the night is not what arrives at the third watch.

Going outside

The only way out of the village is the Courtyard's moon gate — one of its four ordinary ways out, not a fifth. Take it and the Back Steps goes down against the Courtyard, joined along that edge. Then the turn continues as normal.

The gate works both ways. Going back in costs a turn, like any move.

Almost everything you will ever carry is found indoors. Crossing the moon gate is a decision made with what you already have.

Items

You can carry 4. Only talismans stack — however many of one you pile up they share a single slot; everything else takes a slot each, so the three Sticky Rice you start with already fill three of your four slots. You begin the night nearly full, and the first real decision the game asks is what to eat or drop to make room.

Your weapon and your Protective Charm are not in the pack at all. They are in your hands — one each, right and left — and neither costs a slot. Everything else — talismans, medicines, the banner — is used once and is gone.

Weapons — one hand, one blade

A weapon is your Attack, not a bonus on top of it. You begin bare-handed, at 0, and you can hold one blade at a time. There is no carrying several and counting the best.

WeaponAttack
Precept Knife1
Peachwood Sword1
Coin Sword2
Seven-Star Sword3

Find a better blade while you are already holding one and the game stops and asks. Whichever one you turn down stays on the floor of that room — there is one of each in the village and you have no reason to walk back — so the choice is permanent either way, and a True Fire Talisman burned into a blade leaves with that blade. Both attack numbers are put in front of you before you answer, because a burned-in talisman can make the worse steel the better weapon.

There is only one of each weapon in the village. Turn up the one already in your hand and you find nothing. Seven-Star Sword is rare on purpose: roughly one search in seven. Many nights you will fight with something worse, and that is the game working, not the game cheating you.

Talismans — burned once

A talisman adds to your sword. Bring both to the same fight and the two stack.

TalismanWhat it does
True Fire TalismanFight at 1 — or burn it into the blade in your hand for +1, which the blade keeps
Five Thunder TalismanFight at 4
Blood TalismanFight at 5. Writing it costs you 1 Health
CinnabarCopy a talisman you already hold — +2 more of it, all sharing one slot

The True Fire Talisman is the only one worth spending outside a fight. Hold a blade, open the pack and press Into the blade: the paper goes into the steel, the sword is better for the rest of the night, and it costs you no turn. A blade takes one only — so there is a ceiling on how good steel alone ever gets, and Cinnabar cannot copy its way past it.

Everything else is spent in a fight and nowhere else. Talismans are the one thing the village never runs out of — the same one can be found again and again, and a talisman search comes up empty only one time in ten — so they are the cheapest Attack in the game and the only kind you can stockpile.

Health stops at 10. Anything that would take you past it is wasted — so a sticky rice at 9 Health is worth holding rather than eating.

The charm — kept

Protective Charm — jiangshi wounds are 1 less, all night. Nothing else. It is not found by searching: the only way to get one is to save the villager in the 9 PM band, which makes it the earliest opportunity in the game and the one that pays off latest.

The banner — used once

Soul-Snatching Banner — doubles your sword for one fight. It does nothing for a talisman.

There is exactly one place it is ever found: Earth God Shrine the Earth God Shrine, and only sometimes, when you search there. Doubling a sword is wasted on a small pack — damage only goes down to zero, and a sword and talisman together often get you there anyway. Keep it for something big.

The two rites

The Sealed Crypt and the Mass Grave are the only rooms that ask for something beyond walking in.

Sealed Crypt. Resolve the room's event as usual — then one more, which is the opening of the coffin itself. Survive it and still be standing there, and the ancestral tablet is yours. Carrying it does not use an item slot.

Mass Grave. The same shape, for the burial: the room's event, then one more for the digging. Survive that holding the ancestral tablet and you have won.

Both are two events in one turn, and the second comes at the worst possible moment — standing over the hole with the tablet in your hands. Neither win is free.

Midnight

When turn 30 ends the third watch sounds, and Jiangshi King the King comes to whatever place you are standing in.

There is one exchange. He strikes once, you meet it once, and the night is decided. No fleeing, no medicine, no second chance — and Black Dog Blood does not work on him, because there is nowhere he is not.

Only what you are holding decides it. Nothing you did earlier counts here except what it left in your hands.

There is nowhere he does not come. No room refuses him and no ground turns him back — the appointment is kept wherever you are standing when the last card is drawn.

You do not kill him. Nobody destroys a Jiangshi King — the most anyone has ever managed is to put one back.

Winning & losing

EndingHow
the burial — the burialTake the ancestral tablet from the Sealed Crypt, carry it out through the moon gate, and finish the rite at the Mass Grave. It ends the night the moment you finish it
LostHealth reaches 0 — in a fight, from an event, or from poison at the start of a turn

The burial is the win you plan for. It asks for turns spent walking: three particular rooms have to turn up, and the Courtyard is the only way between the village and the hillside.

Midnight is the other way the night can end, and it asks for the opposite — turns spent standing still, and whatever you were able to find while you stood there. The game will not tell you whether what you are carrying is enough. That is the point of it.

There is no losing to the clock.
